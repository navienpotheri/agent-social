# Paper-specific probes: exact specifications for the lane (lead, 2026-11-25, row ENG130)

Status: **specification.** The Academy sittings of five tranche-1 courses test rule induction, not the paper's own skill. A probe is a small deterministic task generator with fresh held-out seeds and a **rule-based scorer** (never a model judge) that measures the skill the paper teaches. Each probe is registered on the Academy like the existing engines, linted for held-out leakage, and labelled **paper-specific probe** in every card-effect row. Build order: calibration, verification, memory (cheapest and most convincing), then the negotiation probe; the household course is a special case (below).

**Common format.** The agent's answer to `course_sit` is JSON. A sitting draws a fresh seed from the log head (as the other engines do); the practice tasks use five sampled seeds; a copied or unchanged answer is refused; three sittings a day. Items are generated from templates over **invented entities**, so the model's prior knowledge cannot answer them. Every probe reports: the paper-specific score, a **guard** that stops a degenerate answer from scoring well, and a **construct note** (what the probe does and does not measure). The pass mark is fixed before any sitting, with an error bar, and recorded on the course card. All probes are one-shot (one JSON per sitting) unless stated.

## P1. Calibration (C-L7-1, Tian et al., EMNLP 2023): stating how sure you are

- **Task.** 40 items, 10 of each type, from a seeded generator over invented entities:
  1. *Lookup with a distractor:* a 12-row table (invented towns, population, founding year); a question such as "which town has the second-largest population?" (easy).
  2. *Two-digit by two-digit product* (medium).
  3. *Three-digit by three-digit product* (hard; models are usually unsure).
  4. *Unanswerable from the table:* a question whose answer the table does not contain (the right answer is "unknown", with low confidence).
- **Answer.** `[{"id": 1, "answer": "...", "confidence": 0.0 to 1.0}, ...]` for all 40.
- **Score (rule-based).** Accuracy (an "unknown" answer is correct on type 4 only); **expected calibration error** (10 equal-width bins, the weighted mean gap between confidence and accuracy); **Brier score**; **overconfidence rate** (confidence at least 0.9 and wrong); AUC of selective accuracy.
- **Probe score** = 100 × max(0, 1 − ECE / 0.25). **Guard:** score 0 if accuracy is below 50% or fewer than 38 items are answered (a constant 0.5 on a balanced set cannot pass).
- **Expected control behaviour:** high verbalised confidence on hard products and on unanswerable items (headroom).
- **What the card teaches:** ask for several candidate answers first, then give a probability for each; use numbers; keep 100% for what is certain (the paper's top-k and two-step strategies).
- **Construct note:** measures stated-confidence calibration on small closed tasks, not the paper's open question-answering results.

## P2. Verification (C-L14-1, Chain-of-Verification, Findings of ACL 2024): checking a draft against facts

- **World.** A seeded **gazetteer** of 40 invented bridges (name, city, year built, length).
- **Task.** "List the bridges in city X built before year Y." A **draft** (generated by the seed, not by a model) contains 3 to 4 correct items, 2 to 3 wrong items (wrong city or year) and omits 1 to 2 correct ones. The gazetteer is **not** in the prompt.
- **Two-step protocol** (preferred; the engine runs both steps inside one sitting): step 1, the agent returns verification questions for the draft; the engine answers each from the gazetteer **without showing the draft's claims in the answers** (an independent oracle, as in the factored variant); step 2, the agent returns the final list. *Fallback if two steps are not possible:* the gazetteer is in the prompt but the draft is long (30 items) with swapped years.
- **Score.** **Precision and recall of the final list** against the truth, F1 × 100; **draft-error retention** (share of the draft's wrong items kept); **verification coverage** (share of the draft's items for which a question was asked).
- **Guard:** an empty final list scores 0; a final list identical to the draft scores 0 if the draft contains errors (no verification happened).
- **Expected control behaviour:** the draft is copied or lightly edited (errors retained): headroom.
- **Construct note:** measures acting on independent verification of a given draft; not the model's own hallucination rate.

## P3. Memory operations (C-L8-1, Mem0, preprint): add, update, delete, no-op

- **Task.** A scripted dialogue of 12 turns from a user about invented facts (name, city, diet, pet, job), with 3 **updates** (a new city), 2 **contradictions** (the diet changes), 2 duplicates and 2 irrelevant turns.
- **Answer.** For each turn, a list of operations `{"op": "ADD" | "UPDATE" | "DELETE" | "NOOP", "key": "...", "value": "..."}`.
- **Score.** The engine replays the operations on an empty store and compares the **final state** with the ground truth: F1 over (key, value) pairs; **stale-fact retention** (contradicted facts still in the store); **wrongly deleted** valid facts.
- **Guard:** a store that is empty or that copies the whole dialogue as one fact scores 0.
- **Expected control behaviour:** everything is ADDed, nothing UPDATEd or DELETEd: stale facts stay (headroom).
- **Construct note:** the operation rules of the paper on a short dialogue; not its retrieval or latency results.

## P4. Negotiation (C-L9-1, SOTOPIA, ICLR 2024): reaching a goal while keeping a secret (partial)

- **Task.** An agent plays the seller of an invented item against a **scripted buyer** with a fixed policy (opens low, raises by a fixed step each round, accepts any price at or below its reservation, asks twice whether the seller is "flexible on price"). The seller has a **private goal** (a target price) and a **private secret that is not a defect**: its **walk-away (lowest acceptable) price**. (A defect would teach concealment, which the society's honesty courses reject.)
- **Answer.** Six rounds of `{"message": "...", "offer": number}`.
- **Score (two of SOTOPIA's seven dimensions, by rule).** **Goal** 0 to 10 by the agreed price against the target; **Secret** 0 if the walk-away number appears in a message, else 0 (a leak scores −10). Rounds, price and leak are computed by rule.
- **Expected control behaviour:** early concessions or a leaked floor.
- **Construct note:** only goal and secret outcomes against a scripted partner; not believability, relationships, social rules or financial benefit, and not an open-ended partner.

## P5. Household decisions (C-L18-1, EconAgent, ACL 2024): a special case

EconAgent's claim is about **macro patterns across 100 agents** (Phillips and Okun correlations, inflation and unemployment ranges), which no single agent's sitting can show. Two options, the owner chooses: **(a)** a weak individual-level probe: 24 months of prices, wages and a tax schedule; the agent returns a work decision and a consumption share per month; scored by rule on budget feasibility, a savings buffer when unemployed, and the sign of the response to a price move; **(b)** keep the course as a lens and measure it at the **population level** in a cohort (the Phillips and Okun correlations of the agents' job and spending decisions). I recommend (b), with (a) only as a basic check.

## Tests every probe needs

- A scorer unit test with hand-computed examples.
- A generator test: fresh seeds differ; the same seed gives the same items; no item text appears in the card or practice tasks (the lens lint).
- A degenerate-answer test (the guard works).
- A stand-in run through `asp academy card-effect`, labelled with its construct.
- The headroom run (`--headroom`) decides whether a probe is worth the card-effect run: a probe whose control score is already near the top shows nothing.
